# Past global winners and finalists in agriculture and Earth observation (2023 to 2025)

Scope: projects that won, reached Global Finalist or got an Honorable Mention in challenges about farming, vegetation, weather for planning, water or land Earth observation. Project details come from the project summaries on the Space Apps site where they are public, and from NASA's winner announcements. For many 2023 and 2024 teams and for the honorable mentions, the project page needs a login or shows nothing, so only the team blurb is recorded. "Why it won" uses the award definitions and the NASA write-ups. The judges' reasons are not published.

Totals: 2025 had 10 winners, 45 finalists and 23 honorable mentions from more than 11,500 submissions. 2024 had 10 winners, 40 finalists (from 940+ nominees) and 19 honorable mentions from 9,996 submissions. 2023 had 10 winners and 40 finalists from 5,500+ teams, with honorable mentions listed alongside.

## Agriculture challenges in past years

| Year | Challenge | What it asked |
|---|---|---|
| 2024 | Leveraging Earth Observation Data for Informed Agricultural Decision-Making | A tool that helps farmers explore and use NASA data on water problems (drought, flood). It named IMERG, OPERA, SAR, Landsat, ASTER, ECOSTRESS, VIIRS, MODIS, SMAP and Data Pathfinders, and called for "farmer-centric" design |
| 2025 | NASA Farm Navigators: Using NASA Data Exploration in Agriculture | An educational game that simulates farming decisions (irrigation, fertilizer, livestock) with real NASA data. It warned about data resolution and soil moisture depth |
| 2026 | Field Shift: Adapting Farms with NASA Data | A crop-rotation decision-support tool |

The agriculture challenge produced 2 of 10 global winners in 2024 and 1 of 10 in 2025.

## Patterns across the winners

These are my reading of the pages, not stated judge reasoning.

- Narrow, concrete decisions win. NVS-knot answered one question (is now a good time to plant?) from two named data sources (SMAP and OpenET). This matches the "Relevance" criterion's demand that NASA data be core.
- A local story counts. QUEÑARIS (Arequipa water) and the Egyptian and Korean farm teams framed their work around a named place and its people. The Local Impact award exists for this.
- Deployed, clickable demos are common. Most 2025 finalists and winners link a live Vercel or similar site or a public repo.
- NASA POWER is the most common data source among 2025 weather finalists. Five of six "Will It Rain" finalists name it, so it alone is unlikely to stand out.
- Strong storytelling and design can win in agriculture (Zumorroda-X, Art & Technology), not only analytics.
- Two agriculture winners in 2024 used water-centred NASA data (soil moisture, ET). For Field Shift, water use per rotation is a likely judged angle.
