# Past Space Apps challenges on farming, food, water and land (2012 to 2025)

Scope: every NASA Space Apps challenge from 2012 to 2025 that we could find about agriculture, food security, soil, crops, drought, irrigation, water for farming, vegetation monitoring or land use. Research date: 2026-11-14. The file `past_winners.md` covers the winning projects. This file covers the challenge statements.

Each entry has a tier:

- **Core** means farming, food or soil is the main subject of the challenge.
- **Related** means farming is one named use, example or user group inside a wider challenge.

Paraphrases are ours. Short quotes are verbatim and under 15 words. Team counts come from the challenge page at the time of capture.

## How we searched

- 2017 to 2022: we downloaded every challenge page from the live year sites (for example https://2021.spaceappschallenge.org/challenges) and scanned the text for farm, crop, food, soil, drought, irrigation, vegetation, NDVI, land cover and land use. We then read every page with hits.
- 2023 to 2025: we read the full challenge lists at https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2023/challenges/, https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/nasa-space-apps-2024/challenges/ and https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2025/challenges/, then read the Details tab of each candidate.
- 2020 COVID-19 edition: we read the full list at https://covid19.spaceappschallenge.org/challenges.
- 2012 to 2016: the old sites are gone or render only with JavaScript. We pulled challenge URL lists from the Wayback Machine CDX API (for example http://web.archive.org/cdx/search/cdx?url=spaceappschallenge.org/challenge/*), picked candidates by title, and read the archived pages. For 2014, 2015 and 2016 we also read the full Earth category list. For 2016 the live site still renders through a browser, so we read it with a headless fetch.

## Per-challenge entries

### 2012

**Growers' Nation** (core)
- URL: http://spaceappschallenge.org/challenge/growers-nation/ (archived at https://web.archive.org/web/20120601000000/http://spaceappschallenge.org/challenge/growers-nation/)
- Objective: build a clickable map that tells people what produce they can grow and when to plant, based on local soil type, recent weather and seasonal outlook. The page title reads "what produce to grow and when given the soil type". It asks for local gardening tips per region.
- NASA data named: none by mission. It asks for weather and climatology data, satellite evapotranspiration and soil maps.
- Asked for: app (web map).

**Pineapple Project** (core)
- URL: http://spaceappschallenge.org/challenge/pineapple-project/ (archived at https://web.archive.org/web/20120601000000/http://spaceappschallenge.org/challenge/pineapple-project/)
- Objective: a website, ideally also a mobile app, that matches rural groups to tropical fruit varieties suited to their region using agricultural, climate and topography data. Solvers got a spreadsheet of temperature, soil, light, elevation and rainfall needs for papaya, mango, guava and pineapple. Two organisations in Rwanda and Nicaragua planned to pilot it.
- NASA data named: none. Sources named are worldclimate.com and FAO.
- Asked for: website and mobile app.

**Commodities Pricing Tool for Rural Communities** (core)
- URL: http://spaceappschallenge.org/challenge/commodities-pricing-tool-rural-communities/ (archived at https://web.archive.org/web/20120601000000/http://spaceappschallenge.org/challenge/commodities-pricing-tool-rural-communities/)
- Objective: a tool for smallholder farmers to contribute and read local food prices, including low-value crops such as cassava, to extend the FAO GIEWS price database. Opportunity International Nicaragua requested it.
- NASA data named: none.
- Asked for: app or crowdsourcing tool.

**Satellite-based estimation of watershed-level evapotranspiration** (related)
- URL: http://spaceappschallenge.org/challenge/satellite-based-estimation-watershed-level-evapotr/ (archived at https://web.archive.org/web/20120601000000/http://spaceappschallenge.org/challenge/satellite-based-estimation-watershed-level-evapotr/)
- Objective: accurate basin-scale evapotranspiration from satellite imagery with field calibration, ideally with online basin water storage for drought and flood planning. Sponsored by Florida International University.
- NASA data named: none by mission.
- Asked for: model and web viewer.

## Analysis

### How the farm ask changed

1. **2012 to 2013: crop choice from soil and climate, with no NASA mission named.** The first year already had two "what should I grow here" tools. Growers' Nation asked what to plant and when from soil type and season (http://spaceappschallenge.org/challenge/growers-nation/). The Pineapple Project matched fruit varieties to region from climate, soil and elevation needs (http://spaceappschallenge.org/challenge/pineapple-project/). Nonprofits and USDA wrote the problems. In 2013 the Soil Testing Kit was built to feed both of those tools with crowdsourced soil data (http://spaceappschallenge.org/challenge/soil-testing-kit/). So the core Field Shift question, "what fits this field", is as old as Space Apps.

2. **2014 to 2016: growers as data sources.** Challenges asked farmers and citizens to collect data: ozone leaf injury photos (https://2014.spaceappschallenge.org/challenge/leaf-me-alone/), pest and disease reports by SMS (https://2015.spaceappschallenge.org/challenge/crop-alert-learning-growers/), and herder routes and water points (https://2016.spaceappschallenge.org/challenges/earth/emobile-pastoralism). They stressed low connectivity and simple, picture-based design. Crop Alert named GLAM and MODIS products, the first strong NASA farm data list we found.

3. **2017 to 2019: SDG framing and the water and soil missions.** Farm topics moved inside sustainability themes. What's for Dinner? and Live Smart cite SDG 2.4 on resilient farming and soil quality (https://2017.spaceappschallenge.org/challenges/earth-and-us/whats-dinner/). Where's the Water? is the first challenge we found that names farmers as users and lists GRACE, GPM and SMAP (https://2017.spaceappschallenge.org/challenges/planetary-blues/wheres-water/). Rise to Resilience! adds ECOSTRESS (https://2019.spaceappschallenge.org/challenges/living-our-world/green-infrastructure-solutions-climate-resilience/). 2018 and 2019 had no core farm challenge.

4. **2020 to 2023: farming as an example, and machine learning arrives.** Apart from the COVID-19 Food for Thought challenge, farming appears only as one use case inside hazard detection, data discovery, heat warnings, water education and foundation-model challenges. 2022 had none. Machine learning becomes an explicit ask in 2020 (https://2020.spaceappschallenge.org/challenges/inform/automated-detection-hazards/), and GeoAI in 2023 asks for metrics and benchmarks (https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2023/challenges/geoai-reimagined-transformative-and-diverse-earth-science-applications-using-foundation-models/?tab=details).

5. **2024 to 2026: a dedicated farm challenge every year, moving toward decision support.** 2024 asked for a farmer-centric water tool and said earlier efforts failed on design and engagement. 2025 asked for a game and, for the first time, warned teams in detail about data limits such as resolution and soil moisture depth. 2026 asks for decision support that compares rotations and explains its evidence, gaps and risks (`../00_challenge/details.md`). The format went from map (2012) to crowdsourcing app (2015) to data story (2017) to model (2020, 2023) to farmer tool (2024) to game (2025) to explainable decision support (2026).

Two threads run through the whole history:

- **Local knowledge next to satellites.** Growers' Nation wanted local gardening tips (2012). Where's the Water? asked teams to combine satellites with local knowledge (2017). The 2024 challenge suggested "Farming Fusion" of field and satellite observations. Field Shift turns this into a rule: the tool supports, and does not replace, farmer judgment.
- **Honesty about method limits.** The 2018 displaced-settlements challenge asked teams to say where and why a method fails (https://2018.spaceappschallenge.org/challenges/what-world-needs-now/land-where-displaced-people-settle/). Farm Navigators asked games to reflect data limits (2025). Field Shift asks for information gaps and uncertainty for each strategy.

### What is new in Field Shift

The comparison uses `../00_challenge/details.md` and the earlier challenge texts listed above.

- **Rotation as the unit of decision.** No earlier challenge we read uses the word "rotation" in a farming sense. We searched every downloaded page from 2012 to 2022 and read the 2023 to 2025 farm pages. Earlier asks were single choices: one crop or fruit for a place (2012), pest alerts in one season (2015), or water risk now (2024). Field Shift asks about a sequence of crops over years.
- **At least three strategies, with the current rotation as baseline.** Earlier tools gave one answer or a dashboard. None asked teams to compare named options against what the farmer does today.
- **An explanation card for each strategy.** Field Shift asks why a strategy was suggested, what evidence supports it, what it may gain, where data is missing, and which risks need local review. Before 2026 the closest asks were the brief explanations in the 2021 heat challenge and the data-limit warnings in 2025.
- **Farmer priorities as input.** Field Shift lists water use, forage value, nitrogen fixing, erosion, organic matter and market fit. Earlier challenges took inputs such as location, farm size or type (2024), soil type (2012) or pest reports (2015), not a weighting of goals.
- **Soil health as the outcome.** Earlier farm challenges aimed at yield, water, pests, prices or food access. Soil appears as an input (2012, 2013) or as an SDG target (2017). Field Shift makes soil health and resilience the goal.
- **SI as a named method, with uncertainty and data harmonisation.** Machine learning appeared from 2020, but never on a farm decision. Field Shift asks teams to compare SI or statistical methods, explain how the model treats uncertainty, and resample SMAP, ECOSTRESS, GPM, Landsat or MODIS, GEDI and soil data to one grid.
- **A wider data list.** NASA POWER, SRTM and GEDI appear for the first time in a farm challenge we read. GRACE and SMAP date back to 2017.
