# NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026: event facts

## Dates

The 2026 hackathon runs on 14 and 15 November 2026 under the theme "The Next Frontier". Today (13 November 2026) is the day before it starts.

The FAQ (dated 5 November 2026) gives looser dates ("December 2026" for nominees, finalists and honorable mentions, "January 2027" for winners). The Judging and Awards Guide (dated 12 November 2026) gives the exact dates above.

"Local time" means the time zone of the team's Local Event. For the Universal Event it means the Team Owner's local time.

## Format

- Two-day hackathon held at hundreds of Local Events worldwide. Local Events can be in person or virtual.
- The Universal Event is a virtual, asynchronous option for people with no Local Event nearby.
- Participation is free. The FAQ says to report anyone who asks for money.
- Teams have at most 6 members. Local Event teams need at least 1 member and Universal Event teams need at least 2 to be eligible for global judging. The guide calls 4 to 5 members ideal.
- Every member must be registered for the same Local Event (not waitlisted) or the Universal Event.
- Each person can be on one team. Each team works on one challenge.
- Teams must not start work on their project before 14 November, 09:00 local time.
- Teams are not expected to finish a fully developed product in two days.
- Space Apps Connect is the official forum, open from 3 November to the end of the hackathon. NASA navigators and subject matter experts answer challenge and data questions there.

## Scale

- 2026 has 14 challenges. Field Shift is rated Intermediate and Advanced.
- The site-wide Find a Team page showed 27,270 teams on 13 November 2026.
- The Field Shift Teams tab showed 1,605 teams on 13 November 2026 (see teams_tab.md).
- For comparison, the 2025 event drew more than 114,000 participants, 551 local events in 167 countries and territories, and more than 11,500 project submissions. The 2024 event drew 93,520 registered participants, 485 events in 163 countries and territories, and 9,996 submissions.
- The count of 2026 Local Events is not confirmed. The local events page did not return a count.

## Global versus local judging

| Stage | Who judges | Output |
|---|---|---|
| Phase 1, Local | Local judges recruited by each Local Lead. Universal Event projects are judged by subject matter experts and compared only with other Universal Event projects | Global Nominees, announced 3 December 2026 |
| Phase 2, Global | Expert judges from NASA and partners | Global Finalists and Honorable Mentions, announced 17 December 2026 |
| Phase 3, Executive | Executive committee from NASA and the 2026 space agency partners | 10 Global Winners, announced 26 January 2027 |

Local Leads may give their own local awards, which are separate from global judging. The FAQ says the Global Nominee selection rate is similar for Local Events and the Universal Event. The number of Universal Event nominees is proportional to the number advancing from Local Events. One Romanian Local Event told press that at least three projects from Bucharest and Cluj-Napoca will advance, with the exact number set by how many projects register there.

## Global Collaborators (free tools for participants)

GoDaddy, Google, Meteomatics, Microsoft and Miro. Their "Global Offers" went live on 13 November.

## Recognition

Every participant whose team submits a project gets a downloadable certificate. Global Winners have been invited to a NASA center or facility for a "Winners Celebration" (named in the terms). Details for 2026 winners are "to be announced", according to the FAQ.
