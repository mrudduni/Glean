# Rules, judging and submission requirements (2026)

## Judging criteria

The Judging and Awards Guide (dated 12 November 2026) names five criteria. Judges score each from 1 to 5 at every level, from Local Event judging to Executive judging. The official names are:

1. Impact
2. Creativity
3. Validity
4. Relevance
5. Presentation

The Project Submission Guide lists them as "Impact, Creativity, Validity, Relevance, and Presentation". The guide's descriptions, summarized:

| Criterion | What earns a 5 |
|---|---|
| Impact | Addresses a significant problem, reaches a large audience or "could change the world", and far exceeds expectations |
| Creativity | A new approach not tried before, or a meaningful improvement on existing ideas, with inventive execution |
| Validity | Scientifically sound and technically feasible, fully developed, ready for real-world use, usable and user-friendly |
| Relevance | Fully addresses the chosen challenge. NASA data is a core part of the solution, not a surface add-on |
| Presentation | Clear, well structured, engaging story about the problem, the solution and why it matters. Judges score the demo and the full project page |

The guide says teams often neglect Presentation, and it advises giving it equal time and recruiting people with storytelling and design skills.

## Award categories (10 Global Awards)

| Award | Definition (summarized) |
|---|---|
| Best Use of Science | Best and most valid use of science or the scientific method |
| Best Use of Data | Best makes space data accessible or uses it for a unique application |
| Best Use of Technology | Most innovative use of technology |
| Galactic Impact | Most potential to improve life on Earth or in the universe |
| Best Mission Concept | Most plausible concept and design |
| Most Inspirational | "The project that captures our hearts" |
| Best Use of Storytelling | Most creatively communicates the potential of open data through storytelling |
| Global Connection | Best connects people around the world through technology |
| Art & Technology | Most effectively combines technical and creative skills |
| Local Impact | Greatest potential to make a difference in the team's local community |

There are also Honorable Mentions at the Global Finalist stage. In 2024 there was also a "People's Choice" badge, but 2026 People's Choice details are not confirmed.

## Submission requirements

Submission happens on the Project tab of the team page. It opens 14 November at 09:00 and closes 15 November at 23:59, local time of the team's Local Event (Universal Event: Team Owner's local time). Late saves are not judged. Teams can resubmit until the deadline, and only the last saved submission is judged.

Required project page fields:

| Field | Notes |
|---|---|
| Project name | |
| High-level summary | What you built, how it meets the challenge, why it matters |
| Project demo link | Either a video of 30 seconds or less, or a slide deck of up to 7 slides including title and intro slides. Going over either limit makes the project ineligible for global judging. Hosted externally (YouTube, Google Drive, GitHub, OneDrive, Dropbox) with a public link and no login |
| Final project link | Public link to a website, repo or files, with no password, permission or registration |
| Project details | What it does, how it works, benefits, goals, tools, languages, hardware and software |
| SI (AI) use | How "Super Intelligence" tools were used (see below) |
| NASA data | Specific NASA data and resources used. At least one NASA data source or resource is required for a Global Award |
| Space agency partner data | Encouraged, not required |
| References | Every external resource, including open-source code, text and images |

Other rules:

- All content must be in English. A demo video may use another language only with English subtitles. Slides must be in English.
- Images (JPG, PNG, GIF up to 10 MB) can be pasted on the page. Videos and documents must be linked.
- The guide suggests requesting a FortiGuard Web Filter Classification for a new project website, so judges' networks do not block it. This is optional.
- No images, video, audio or likeness of anyone under 18 in the demo or project.
- Two checkboxes must be ticked. One agrees to the submission guide, the Terms and Conditions and the Privacy Policy. The other confirms the work is original, that every external resource is listed in References, and that no copyrighted material was used without permission.
- Suggested 30-second video outline: 0:00 names, 0:02 challenge and inspiration, 0:05 project title and tagline, 0:10 how it works and which NASA and partner data make it possible, 0:20 to 0:30 the future vision.
- Suggested slide contents: team name, member names, chosen challenge, an image of the project, the user experience, and how NASA and partner data make it possible.
- A repository link is not named as a required field. The final project link can be a GitHub repo.

## AI use rules ("Super Intelligence")

The FAQ says that, following a 29 September 2026 U.S. Executive Order, the site uses "Super Intelligence (SI)" in place of "Artificial Intelligence (AI)". The rules:

- SI tools (LLMs, chatbots, image and video generators, code generators, foundation models) are allowed to speed up development.
- The Project Submission Guide says SI should not be used to come up with the team's idea for how to solve the challenge.
- Each submission must describe how SI tools were used. The answer to the SI question is for data collection and judges do not see it, but judges do see SI markers inside the project.
- SI-generated images and videos must carry a visible watermark. SI audio must be acknowledged in descriptive text and metadata. SI code and data must be acknowledged in descriptive text and metadata.
- Generated content must not use or modify NASA branding (logos, flags, mission identifiers).

## Eligibility

- Open to all ages. Under-18s must be registered by, and accompanied at all times by, a parent or legal guardian. Local Events may add stricter rules.
- Under-18s must not upload their photo as an avatar or team image, and must not appear in demos.
- U.S. Federal Government civil servants cannot register as participants. Federal contractor staff may join only in their personal capacity, outside work hours, without mentioning the contractor link.
- Participants may not take part through an affiliation with China or a Chinese-owned entity (including Hong Kong and Macau), and may not collaborate with such entities on their project. The test is affiliation, not nationality. NASA screens teams before global judging.
- Universal Event teams need at least 2 members for global judging.
- All team members must be on the Members tab and registered for the same event before submission closes. Members cannot be added after submission closes.

## IP and licensing terms

- By submitting, the team lets NASA display, copy, combine, compile, distribute and disseminate the solution to support Space Apps.
- All original content must be freely available without restriction or licensed as open source under an OSI-approved licence. If no licence is named, NASA distributes the original content under Apache 2.0.
- Non-NASA materials (datasets or code not from NASA or partner agencies) must be freely and publicly available, or OSI open-source licensed. Their licences must be named in the documentation, or NASA will not post the solution.
- Every source must be cited, even open-source ones. NASA can remove nominations, awards or winner status for missing citations.
- Teams must make sure their materials contain no export-controlled information (ITAR or EAR).
- Submissions may not include proprietary or business-sensitive information the team cannot make public, commercial promotion, personal data, or material the team does not hold rights to.
- Submitting grants NASA a publicity right to use names, place of residence, country, likeness and project information. Video submissions grant the U.S. government a royalty-free, worldwide, perpetual, non-exclusive licence.

For this project, this means GAEZ, Sentinel-2, ERA5 and every USDA or other non-NASA dataset must have its licence stated in the submission. The code repo should carry an OSI licence (Apache 2.0 by default).
