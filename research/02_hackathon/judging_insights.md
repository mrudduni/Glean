# Judging insights for Field Shift 2026

This file adds to `rules_judging.md`, which already covers the five criteria, the score-5 wording, the ten awards, the submission fields, the 30-second video outline and the SI rules. Read that file first. This file covers how judges actually work, how nominees are picked, what past winning demos showed, and what judges say they look for.

## How judges are told to score

The 2025 judge instructions (the "Final Space Apps 2025 Judging Guidelines", a PDF written for judges, not participants) add three things that the 2026 participant guide does not say (https://spaceapps-v2-staging.s3.amazonaws.com/media/documents/Final_Space_Apps_2025_Judging_Guidelines_for_web.pdf). The 2026 judge version is not public, so treat these as likely, not confirmed, for 2026.

1. **A pass or fail gate comes first.** If a project fails any minimum requirement, the judge scores it 1 and stops. The gate lists: mainly in English, answers the chosen challenge, demo within 7 slides or 30 seconds, every link open without a password, NASA open data used, and SI use declared with watermarks on generated images and video and no altered NASA branding.
2. **Scores are holistic.** Judges rate each project from 1 to 5 overall, and the criteria are "guidance". A notes field lets them jot per-criterion scores if they want.
3. **Global judges are told to be selective** and to give a 5 only to "truly exemplary" projects. Executive judges pick awards using the award descriptions.

The guidelines also describe what a 3 and a 1 look like, which the participant guide leaves out. In short:

| Criterion | A 3 looks like | A 1 looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Impact | Useful ideas for a moderate audience, but reach and execution could grow | No meaningful problem addressed, little benefit to anyone |
| Creativity | Some original parts, but the approach "feels somewhat familiar" | Relies on established ideas with no fresh angle |
| Validity | Reasonable science, but parts need refinement or testing, and usability could improve | No strong scientific basis, big gaps, incomplete |
| Relevance | Fits the challenge in general, but NASA data is supplementary, meaning "the method could function without it" | NASA data is absent or superficial |
| Presentation | Clear enough, but lacks depth or a compelling story | Confusing, weak or disjointed |

For Field Shift this means the Relevance test is concrete: a judge will ask whether the tool would still work if the NASA layers were removed. A rotation engine that only uses NASA POWER as a weather lookup risks a 3.

The 2025 judging calendar ran on fixed deadlines after the 4 to 5 October 2025 event: local judging by 12 October, Universal Event judging by 20 October, global judging by 10 November and executive judging by 30 November (same PDF). Local judges therefore had about a week. The 2026 equivalents are not published, but nominees are announced on 3 December 2026 (see `event.md`).

## The demo is read first

The 2021 Project Demo Guide says the demo "is often the first thing judges review" (https://sa-2019.s3.amazonaws.com/media/documents/Space_Apps_2021_Project_Demo_Guide.pdf). It advises strong visuals, starting the demo early, a teammate who owns storytelling, and testing that links open without a login. Its "30 Seconds of Glory" timeline is the same one the 2026 submission guide uses (see `rules_judging.md`). The guide frames the goal as reaching "someone who has no context, technical background, or previous knowledge" of the project.

The 2025 judge gate also checks demo length. A 31-second video fails the gate and scores 1 (2025 judging guidelines, above).

## How Global Nominees are picked

| Question | Answer | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Who picks at a Local Event? | A panel of local judges that the Local Lead recruits, trains and organizes. They use the official criteria | https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/resources/judging-awards-guide/ |
| How many per Local Event? | The 2026 guide gives no number. The 2022 guide said 1 to 3 per location, depending on how many projects were submitted there. The 2017 blog said two Global Nominees plus one People's Choice nominee per location. Wikipedia says the number depends on submissions per event | https://spaceapps-prod.s3.amazonaws.com/media/documents/Space_Apps_2022_Judging_and_Awards_Guide.pdf, https://2017.spaceappschallenge.org/blog/how-develop-winning-space-apps-solution, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA_International_Space_Apps_Challenge |
| Overall rate | 2025 had 1,294 Global Nominees ("1,290+") from more than 11,500 submissions, about 11%. 2024 had 940+ nominees from 9,996 submissions, about 9% (see `past_winners.md`). The 2022 guide said the Universal Event rate is "usually around one in ten" | https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2025/awards/global-nominees/, 2022 guide above |
| Universal Event | Judged by subject matter experts (about 40 in 2022), compared only with other Universal Event projects, with a nominee count proportional to the Local Event rate. The 2026 FAQ says the rate is "comparable" and "equally competitive" | 2026 judging guide and FAQ, 2022 guide |
| Are local awards the same as nominations? | No. Local awards are separate, and a local prize winner is not always the team that advances | 2026 judging guide, 2022 guide |
| Local formats differ | Seoul runs local judging, then local awards, then a recommendation of Global Nominee candidates. HackAthlone gave each team 3 minutes to pitch and 2 minutes of questions in 2025, and nominated four Athlone projects that year. Bangladesh used a 240-second pitch video for the local round in 2023 | https://nasaspaceappskr.org/2026/en/bulletin/project-journey-seoul-to-global, https://hackathlone.com/blogs/you-dont-need-to-code-to-win-at-space-apps, https://tbsgraduates.net/campus/campus-hero/how-to-craft-a-winning-story-in-conversation-with-team-voyagers |

The practical point: the first gate is a local panel that may watch a live pitch or a longer local video, while global judges see only the 30-second demo and the project page. A team needs both. Ask the Local Lead what the local format is.

## What judges and winners say

- **A 2026 local judge in Bucharest** (an engineering manager at Stripe) told press he is "not looking for the most polished pitch". He looks for teams that start with a problem, test ideas against real data, and explain why the work matters, and he says data is what makes a project hold up beyond the demo (https://business-review.eu/tech/it/innovation-feasibility-and-relevance-among-the-criteria-for-evaluating-projects-at-nasa-space-apps-challenge-302778).
- **Team Voyagers** (2023 Global Winner, Best Storytelling) read the guidelines and mark distribution closely, assumed judges have little time per project, and so put their effort into a tight script for the 30-second video. They also chose a challenge that suited their storytelling strength rather than their subject knowledge (https://tbsgraduates.net/campus/campus-hero/how-to-craft-a-winning-story-in-conversation-with-team-voyagers).
- **A 2021 CSA Space Apps winner** advises building on existing tools instead of starting from scratch, and says a team still earns points if it can explain its ideas when the build does not fully work (https://www.asc-csa.gc.ca/eng/blog/2022/09/22/seven-tip-sto-win-the-space-apps-challenge.asp).
- **A 2024 local winner in Ayacucho** reflected that polishing the landing page ate time that a less polished build could have given to the pitch (https://kateincoding.medium.com/how-we-became-local-winners-at-the-nasa-space-apps-challenge-2024-in-ayacucho-b8580aafb4a8).
- **HackAthlone organizers** say judges hear the pitch and watch the demo, and most of what makes those good happens away from the keyboard. They name an agriculture student on a Field Shift team as the kind of member who spots problems a programmer will not (https://hackathlone.com/blogs/you-dont-need-to-code-to-win-at-space-apps).

Patterns across these demos: each answers one plain question a user asks, shows a real place, and shows the product on screen rather than slides of text. The two agriculture data winners (WaterWise and 2plant) each turn one expert variable into one recurring farm decision and show their method.

## Changes on the official pages since the 13 November notes

- The Judging and Awards Guide and the Participant FAQs are now dated 13 November 2026. `rules_judging.md` cites the guide as dated 12 November.
- Both pages now say Global Winners will be invited to a virtual Winner's Celebration in early 2027 (https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/resources/judging-awards-guide/, https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/resources/participant-faqs/). `event.md` says winners have been invited to a NASA center. For 2026, the official pages say virtual.
- The FAQ states that teams "are not allowed to begin working on the challenges prior to the hackathon". Many public competitor repos have commits from September and October (see `competition_2026.md`). How judges enforce this is not confirmed.

## What this means for our submission

- Clear the gate first: a demo of 30 seconds or less, every link open without login, English, SI use declared, watermarks on any generated media.
- Make NASA data impossible to remove. The 3-versus-5 line on Relevance is whether the method works without it.
- Spend real time on the 30-second video. It is read first, and the Presentation score covers the demo and the page together.
- Answer one farmer question on screen, in one real place, and show the evidence behind the answer, including how accurate the data is against ground truth.
- Ask our Local Lead how local judging runs (live pitch, length, questions), since that panel decides whether global judges ever see the project.
