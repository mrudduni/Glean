## 1. Ogallala aquifer depletion in Kansas and Texas

### Summary

The High Plains aquifer has lost about 286 million acre-feet of recoverable storage since irrigation began. Kansas measures about 1,400 wells each January. Water levels in southwest Kansas fell by more than a foot in 2024. In 2025 the state average rose 0.2 ft for the first time since 2019, thanks to rain, but the 30-year trend is still downward. The Sheridan 6 Local Enhanced Management Area (SD-6 LEMA) is the best-studied case of farmers choosing to pump less. It cut water use by 25 to 31 percent while incomes held. Most of the saving came from more efficient irrigation, and a smaller part came from switching corn and soybean to sorghum and wheat. Kansas law HB 2279 (2023) required every groundwater district to file an action plan by 1 July 2026. In September 2026 the chief engineer rejected the water-level part of the southwest district's plan. In Texas, farmers near Lubbock are moving to dryland cotton and sorghum, and crop insurance rules slow the switch.

### Key numbers

- The aquifer's area-weighted water level fell 16.5 ft from predevelopment to 2019, and storage fell by 286.4 million acre-feet to 2.91 billion acre-feet. Source: USGS, https://www.usgs.gov/publications/water-level-and-recoverable-water-storage-changes-high-plains-aquifer-predevelopment-1, 13 Feb 2024.
- Kansas 2025 measurements showed an average rise of about 0.2 ft, the first since 2019. The 1996 to 2025 average is a fall of 0.57 ft per year, and the southwest district (GMD3) averages a fall of 1.66 ft per year. Source: Kansas Geological Survey (KGS), https://kgs.ku.edu/news/article/groundwater-levels-in-the-kansas-high-plains-aquifer-see-first-overall-increase-since-2019, 2 Apr 2026.
- From January 2024 to January 2025, GMD3 fell 1.52 ft and northwest GMD4 fell 1.34 ft. KGS estimates one area needs a 17.5 percent pumping cut to hold steady. Source: Kansas Reflector, https://kansasreflector.com/2025/01/28/ogallala-aquifer-drops-by-more-than-a-foot-in-parts-of-western-kansas, 28 Jan 2025.
- Pumping cuts of 18 to 32 percent would hold levels steady for one to two decades in most of the worst areas. In SD-6 the decline slowed from 2.0 ft per year (2002 to 2012) to 0.5 ft per year (2013 to 2022). The district-wide GMD4 LEMA had no significant effect yet. Source: KGS, https://kgs.ku.edu/news/article/2023/12/06/kgs-report-assesses-health-high-plains-aquifer, 6 Dec 2023.
- SD-6 cut water use 31 percent compared with a control area in 2013 to 2017. Of the saving, 72 percent came from efficiency and 19 percent from a shift to lower-water crops. Source: Deines et al. 2019, Environmental Research Letters, https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1748-9326/aafe39 (abstract only).
- SD-6 cut use by 25 percent and slowed depletion by 65 percent while farm incomes held. Source: Orduña Alegría et al., Nature Sustainability, https://www.nature.com/articles/s41893-024-01437-0, 7 Oct 2024 (abstract only).
- SD-6 replaced corn and soybean with sorghum and wheat and cut blue water use 28 percent, fossil energy 22 percent, and greenhouse gas 20 percent, with yield losses of 1.2 to 15 percent. Source: Castaño-Sánchez et al. 2025, Agricultural Water Management, https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0378377425000848 (abstract only).
- A cut in pumping does not equal the same gain in the water balance. In SD-6, only 22 percent of a 25 percent extraction cut improved the net balance, because return flow to the aquifer also fell. Source: Deines et al. 2021, Water Resources Research (abstract only, via Consensus).
- Irrigation is 85 percent of Kansas water use. Source: Kansas Reflector, https://kansasreflector.com/2024/06/13/time-for-a-reckoning-kansas-farmers-brace-for-water-cuts-to-save-ogallala-aquifer, 13 Jun 2024. Some areas have about 50 years of water left. Source: Kansas Public Radio, https://kansaspublicradio.org/2024-12-11/western-kansas-farmers-are-wrestling-with-how-to-save-their-water-source-and-their-future, 11 Dec 2024.
- Canola needs about 40 percent less water than corn, according to farmers interviewed by NPR. Source: https://npr.org/2025/07/01/nx-s1-5405455/great-plains-farmers-consider-switching-crops-as-aquifer-runs-out-of-water, 1 Jul 2025.
- GMD3's water-level plan was rejected on 30 September 2026. GMD2 and GMD5 plans were approved. Source: High Plains Journal op-ed by Kansas chief engineer Earl Lewis, https://hpj.com/2026/11/07/groundwater-level-declines-impact-states-future, 7 Nov 2026.
- Converting all irrigated Texas High Plains land to dryland would cost more than $1.6 billion a year in output and about 7,300 jobs. One farmer went from more than 80 percent irrigated to 10 percent, rotating cotton and sorghum. Source: Texas Monthly reprint, https://texasgroundwater.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/As-the-Ogallala-Aquifer-Dwindles-West-Texas-Farmers-Face-Irrigation-Issues.pdf, Aug 2024.

### Affected regions

Kansas GMD3 (southwest, worst), GMD1 (Wichita County), and GMD4 (Sheridan and Thomas counties). The Texas Panhandle and South Plains (Hale, Swisher, Lubbock). The Oklahoma Panhandle, eastern Colorado, and eastern New Mexico.

### Where a rotation tool helps and where it cannot

The tool can compare corn and soybean against sorghum, wheat, canola, cotton, and limited-irrigation rotations by water use per acre. It can test a plan against the KGS cut targets of 17.5 to 32 percent or a LEMA allocation. It can also warn that efficiency gains overstate real aquifer gains. The tool cannot set water rights or allocations, enforce LEMAs, or change crop insurance rules that favour irrigated crops. It cannot see well yield or depth to water, which need KGS well data.

### NASA evidence link

GRACE and GRACE-FO show regional storage trends but cannot see single fields. We found no strong 2023 to 2026 GRACE paper specific to the High Plains (not confirmed). Landsat gives irrigated area and crop maps, OpenET (built on Landsat) gives field water use, GPM IMERG and NASA POWER give the rainfall that drives pumping, and SMAP gives soil moisture risk for dryland crops.

## 2. Punjab and Haryana groundwater and the rice-wheat rotation

### Summary

Punjab draws 156 percent of its yearly groundwater recharge, the highest rate in India, and Haryana draws 137 percent. Most of that water goes to paddy, which state policy and guaranteed purchase at the minimum support price (MSP) keep profitable. The Punjab Preservation of Subsoil Water Act 2009 delayed rice transplanting. It slowed the decline but also squeezed the gap before wheat sowing, which a study links to more stubble burning. Both states pay farmers to switch from paddy: Punjab pays Rs 17,500 per hectare for kharif maize, and Haryana pays Rs 8,000 per acre under Mera Pani Meri Virasat (MPMV). These schemes move small areas. Haryana's paddy area grew 35 percent from 2015 to 2024, and fewer than 20 percent of MPMV's 2025 target acres qualified. NASA GRACE data first showed this depletion in 2009 and later studies confirm it.

### Key numbers

- Punjab 2025: extraction 26.27 billion cubic metres (BCM) against 16.80 BCM extractable, a stage of 156.36 percent. Of 153 blocks, 111 are over-exploited. Source: Lok Sabha answer annexure, https://sansad.in/getFile/loksabhaquestions/annex/187/AS200_gYYvwv.pdf?source=pqals, 12 Feb 2026.
- India 2025: stage of extraction 60.63 percent, with 730 of 6,762 units over-exploited. Haryana is at 136.75 percent. Source: PIB, https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2206073, 18 Dec 2025.
- Punjab 2024: 156.87 percent, with 26.24 BCM of 27.66 BCM going to irrigation, and Sangrur district at 313 percent. Haryana 2024: 135.96 percent, 88 of 143 units over-exploited. Agriculture uses 87 percent of India's groundwater draw. Source: Hindu BusinessLine, https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/data-stories/data-focus/punjab-haryana-rajasthan-lead-in-groundwater-overexploitation/article69381032.ece, 27 Mar 2025.
- Rodell, Velicogna and Famiglietti (2009) used GRACE to measure a loss of 4.0 ± 1.0 cm per year (17.7 km³ per year) over Rajasthan, Punjab, Haryana, and Delhi, a net 109 km³ from August 2002 to October 2008. Source: Nature 460:999, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19675570, Aug 2009 (abstract read; confirmed).
- GRACE and GRACE-FO show north India groundwater falling about 1.5 cm per year, a net 450 km³ over 2002 to 2021. Monsoon rain fell about 8 percent from 1951 to 2021. Source: Mishra et al. 2024, Earth's Future (abstract only, via Consensus).
- GRACE with GLDAS shows Haryana depletion of about 11.3 mm per year over 2003 to 2023, steepest in Mahendragarh and Kurukshetra. Source: Nagal et al. 2025, International Journal of Remote Sensing (abstract only, via Consensus).
- Punjab pays Rs 17,500 per hectare to move from paddy to kharif maize. The 2026-27 scheme covers 16 districts with a 20,000 ha target, paid after geo-tagged checks. Source: The Tribune, https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/punjab-govt-expands-kharif-maize-project-to-16-districts/, 4 May 2026.
- Haryana paddy area rose from 13.53 lakh ha (2015) to 18.37 lakh ha (2024). Farmers say paddy earns about Rs 80,000 per acre against at most Rs 50,000 for other crops. Source: Indian Express, https://indianexpress.com/article/cities/chandigarh/haryana-paddy-water-guzzling-govt-alternatives-10135103/, 18 Jul 2025.
- Punjab has 14.5 lakh irrigation tubewells, the water table falls nearly 1 m a year, and paddy covers more than 30 lakh ha. Farmers still grow the banned long-duration PUSA-44 with seed bought in other states. Source: Hindustan Times, https://hindustantimes.com/cities/chandigarh-news/paddy-sowing-from-june-1-punjab-farmers-opting-for-water-guzzling-varieties-a-big-worry-101748373378347.html, 28 May 2025.
- After the 2009 Act, high-rice districts still fell 1.4 to 1.5 m more than low-rice districts. Source: Sustainability, https://mdpi.com/2071-1050/15/3/2426, Jan 2023 (abstract only). A separate study finds the Act delayed and increased residue burning. Source: Economics Letters, https://sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0165176522000969, 2022 (abstract only).
- Punjab farm fires fell from 36,663 (2023) to 10,909 (2024), yet the state's burnt-area estimate rose slightly from 19.14 to 19.17 lakh ha. Officials suggest farmers burn between satellite passes. Source: The Hindu, https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/punjab/data-show-punjab-farm-fires-fell-by-70-in-2024-yet-the-more-accurate-measure-of-total-burnt-area-rose/article70124604.ece, 4 Oct 2025.
- Punjab counted 5,517 farm fires in 2026 up to 11 November. Source: New Indian Express, https://www.newindianexpress.com/india/2026/Nov/12/punjab-farm-fire-cases-rise-to-5517-1259-firs-registered, 12 Nov 2026.

### Affected regions

Punjab (Sangrur, Patiala, Moga, Barnala, Bathinda), Haryana (Kurukshetra, Kaithal, Karnal, Mahendragarh, Sirsa), north Rajasthan, and Delhi.

### Where a rotation tool helps and where it cannot

The tool can compare paddy-wheat with maize-wheat, cotton-wheat, pulses, and direct-seeded rice using water budgets, sowing windows, and the stated state payments. It can flag burning risk when late paddy harvest leaves little time before wheat. The tool cannot change MSP purchase guarantees, free farm power, weak maize markets, notified transplanting dates, or seed bans. These policy factors explain most of the gap between advice and practice.

### NASA evidence link

GRACE and GRACE-FO give the regional storage decline (scale of about 300 km, not field scale). SMAP gives root-zone moisture, GPM IMERG gives monsoon deficits, MODIS, ECOSTRESS, and OpenET-style ET give crop water use, and MODIS and VIIRS active fire give burning counts that miss fires between passes. Landsat gives burnt area and crop type.

## 3. US Midwest corn-soybean monoculture and soil loss

### Summary

A satellite study found the fertile A horizon gone from about a third of farmed land in the Corn Belt. Nitrogen from the Upper Mississippi basin still drives a Gulf dead zone about twice the federal goal. The 2026 zone was small only because a tropical storm mixed the water before the survey. Cover crops grew 17 percent between the 2017 and 2022 Censuses but cover only 4.7 percent of cropland. The Marsden Farm trial in Iowa shows that 3- and 4-year rotations matched corn-soybean yield and profit while cutting synthetic nitrogen and herbicide by more than 80 percent. Federal support shifted in 2025: the July reconciliation law moved unspent Inflation Reduction Act (IRA) conservation money into the farm bill baseline without the climate focus, NRCS lost about 23 percent of its staff, and USDA cancelled the climate-smart commodities program.

### Key numbers

- The A horizon is fully gone from 35 ± 11 percent of cultivated Corn Belt land. Source: Thaler et al. 2021, PNAS, https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1922375118 (snippet only, page blocked). The yield and dollar losses often quoted with this paper are not confirmed.
- The 2025 Gulf hypoxic zone measured 4,402 square miles. The goal is a five-year average below 1,900 square miles by 2035. Source: NOAA, https://coastalscience.noaa.gov/news/below-average-summer-2025-dead-zone-measured-in-gulf, 31 Jul 2025.
- NOAA forecast 7,027 square miles for 2026, with May nitrate load about 4 percent above average. Source: NOAA, https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/news/jun26/summer-dead-zone.html, 16 Jun 2026.
- The 2026 zone measured 1,332 square miles, second smallest in 40 years, because Tropical Storm Bertha mixed the water. Source: NOAA, https://www.noaa.gov/news-release/noaa-and-partners-find-smaller-than-anticipated-hypoxic-zone-in-gulf-of-america, 5 Aug 2026.
- Marsden Farm, Iowa, 2003 to 2011: corn yields were 12.3 Mg/ha in the 2-year rotation against 12.7 and 12.9 in the 3- and 4-year rotations. Synthetic N fell from 80 to 16 and 11 kg/ha. Herbicide fell 88 percent. Profit did not differ significantly ($953 against $965 and $913 per ha), and the longer rotations needed more labour. Source: Davis et al. 2012, PLOS ONE, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3468434, Oct 2012.
- Cover crops rose from 15.4 million acres (2017) to 18.0 million acres (2022), or 4.7 percent of cropland. Source: USDA ERS, https://ers.usda.gov/data-products/charts-of-note/108950, 16 Apr 2024.
- Total covered cropland fell about 4 percent from 2012 to 2022 because land leaving the Conservation Reserve Program offset cover crop gains. The authors suggest about $50 per acre in support may be needed. Source: farmdoc daily, https://farmdocdaily.illinois.edu/2024/02/cover-crops-and-covered-cropland-2022-us-census-of-agriculture.html, 22 Feb 2024.
- The IRA added about $19.5 billion for conservation. The July 2025 law (P.L. 119-21) rescinded unspent funds, moved them into the permanent baseline, removed the climate-practice requirement, and lowered outlays by $1.8 billion over 10 years. Source: Congressional Research Service, https://congress.gov/crs_external_products/IF/HTML/IF13114.html, 2025.
- NRCS staff fell from nearly 12,000 to just over 9,000 in 2025, a loss of more than 23 percent, and EQIP remains heavily oversubscribed. Source: Inside Climate News, https://insideclimatenews.org/news/08042026/trump-usda-staff-cuts/, 8 Apr 2026.
- USDA cancelled the Partnerships for Climate-Smart Commodities on 14 April 2025. Source: Columbia Sabin Center, https://climate.law.columbia.edu/content/usda-cancels-partnerships-climate-smart-commodities, Apr 2025. The $3.1 billion program size comes from search snippets.

### Affected regions

Iowa, Illinois, Indiana, Minnesota, Ohio, Missouri, Nebraska, and the Dakotas. Downstream, the Louisiana and Texas Gulf fisheries.

### Where a rotation tool helps and where it cannot

The tool can show erosion risk from slope and rainfall intensity and nitrate leaching risk in wet springs. It can lay out Marsden-type trade-offs: less nitrogen and herbicide, similar profit, more labour. It can show the cover crop window a field has and link to EQIP and CSP. It cannot measure topsoil depth or field nitrate loss, which need soil cores and water tests. It cannot fix NRCS staffing. The Marsden results come from one site whose longer rotations used manure, so the tool must say so.

### NASA evidence link

SRTM slope with GPM IMERG rain intensity gives erosion risk. SMAP shows wet-spring leaching risk and cover crop planting windows. Landsat and MODIS show winter green cover and crop history (and Landsat bare-soil brightness is the method Thaler used). NASA POWER gives growing degree days.

## 4. California Central Valley and SGMA

### Summary

The Sustainable Groundwater Management Act (SGMA) requires critically overdrafted basins to reach balance in the early 2040s. The Public Policy Institute of California (PPIC) estimates that 500,000 to 900,000 acres of San Joaquin Valley farmland may leave irrigated production. The State Water Board put Tulare Lake (April 2024) and Tule (September 2024) on probation, while Kern avoided probation in 2025. The state land repurposing program has funded about 4,800 acres, far below PPIC's scale. Almond acreage has fallen four years running. PPIC's 2026 brief promotes low-water winter forage as a crop that fits SGMA. NASA GRACE work shows Central Valley depletion sped up sharply in 2019 to 2021, and the state now uses GRACE and OpenET in its groundwater reporting.

### Key numbers

- 500,000 to 900,000 acres (10 to 20 percent of valley farmland) could leave production by the early 2040s. Alfalfa uses 3.5 to 5 acre-feet per year. Winter forage can establish with 4 inches of extra irrigation, and with 8 inches, 92 percent of locations are marginal or profitable. Source: PPIC, https://ppic.org/publication/policy-brief-the-financial-viability-and-broader-benefits-of-sgma-ready-crops, 24 Jun 2026.
- Even in the best case some 500,000 acres may need fallowing. Source: PPIC, https://ppic.org/publication/managing-water-and-farmland-transitions-in-the-san-joaquin-valley, 18 Sep 2023.
- Tulare Lake became the first basin on probation on 16 April 2024. 698 wells are at risk of going dry, and groundwater supplies about 40 percent of state water in an average year and nearly 60 percent in a dry year. Source: State Water Board, https://waterboards.ca.gov/press_room/press_releases/2024/pr20240415-tulare-lake-probationary-hearing.html, 15 Apr 2024.
- Tule went on probation on 17 September 2024, and canal damage from subsidence is nearly half a billion dollars. Source: State Water Board, https://waterboards.ca.gov/press_room/press_releases/2024/pr240918-tule-probationary-hearing-action.html, 18 Sep 2024.
- The Kern subbasin (1.78 million acres) avoided probation on 17 September 2025. Seven basins have been referred since 2023. Source: State Water Board, https://waterboards.ca.gov/press_room/press_releases/2025/pr20250918-kern-county-subbasin-return-to-dwr.html, 18 Sep 2025.
- The Multibenefit Land Repurposing Program has nearly $80 million allocated and more than 4,800 acres of projects. Source: EDF, https://blogs.edf.org/waterfront/2026/03/10/new-report-california-multibenefit-land-repurposing-program-surpasses-4800-acres-of-projects, 10 Mar 2026.
- Almond acreage fell to 1,505,997 acres in 2025, with 49,197 acres removed that year. Source: Almond Board of California, https://almonds.org/about-us/press-room/california-almond-acreage-declines-fourth-consecutive-year, 19 Nov 2025.
- Central Valley groundwater loss rose from 2.41 km³ per year (2003 to 2021) to 8.58 km³ per year (2019 to 2021). Source: Liu et al., Nature Communications, NASA record https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20220019268, 19 Dec 2022 (abstract only).
- The Department of Water Resources has included GRACE data in its semi-annual groundwater report since May 2024. Source: NASA JPL, https://grace.jpl.nasa.gov/news/217/data-in-action-grace-and-grace-fo-are-used-by-californias-department-of-water-resources-for-groundwat/, 13 Aug 2024.
- A parcel-level Groundwater Accounting Platform that uses satellite data opened statewide in May 2024. Source: EDF, https://edf.org/media/groundwater-accounting-platform-offers-data-driven-solution-american-west, 28 May 2024. OpenET's current role at Rosedale-Rio Bravo is not confirmed.

### Affected regions

The Tulare Lake, Tule, Kaweah, and Kern subbasins (Kings, Tulare, Kern counties), plus Madera, Merced, Delta-Mendota, Chowchilla, and the west side of the San Joaquin Valley.

### Where a rotation tool helps and where it cannot

The tool can compare an annual rotation, winter forage on summer-fallow land, and full fallow by water use and returns. It can show the ET numbers that a groundwater agency will use. It cannot set agency allocations, water trade prices, or surface deliveries. Removing almond or pistachio orchards is a capital decision, not a rotation choice, so the tool fits annual cropland better.

### NASA evidence link

GRACE and GRACE-FO give valley-scale storage, Landsat through OpenET gives field ET for SGMA accounting, ECOSTRESS gives finer-time ET and stress, and InSAR (Sentinel-1 and now NISAR) gives subsidence. InSAR subsidence figures are not confirmed here.

## 5. Climate shifts: heat, seasons, frost, extreme rain, and recent droughts

### Summary

The IPCC finds that warming has already slowed farm productivity growth in mid and low latitudes and made sudden food production losses more frequent. In the US the frost-free season has grown by more than two weeks since 1900. More rain now falls in heavy single events, which drives erosion. Recent droughts show how one bad season can wipe out a grain system: Argentina's 2023 drought nearly halved its soybean crop, and the driest February in 40 years destroyed 69 percent of Zambia's maize area in 2024. In 2025 and 2026, heat and drought cut UK and EU harvests and Kenya's maize crop. India's monsoon swung from 94 percent of normal in 2023 to 108 percent in 2024 and 2025 and to 87 percent in 2026, and its spread across regions mattered as much as the total.

### Key numbers

- About 10 percent of current crop and livestock area becomes climatically unsuitable by 2050 and more than 30 percent by 2100 under very high emissions (SSP5-8.5). Diversification and climate services are rated feasible adaptations. Source: IPCC AR6 WGII Chapter 5, https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg2/downloads/report/IPCC_AR6_WGII_Chapter05.pdf, 2022 (Executive Summary read).
- The US growing season has grown by more than two weeks since about 1900. Since 1980 the last spring frost comes more than 3 days earlier on average. Source: US EPA climate indicators (archived snapshot), https://19january2025snapshot.epa.gov/climate-indicators/climate-change-indicators-length-growing-season, Jun 2024.
- Nine of the top ten years for extreme one-day US rainfall have occurred since 1995. Source: US EPA, https://19january2025snapshot.epa.gov/climate-indicators/climate-change-indicators-heavy-precipitation, Jun 2024.
- The heaviest 1 percent of daily rainfall events in the US Northeast rose about 60 percent. Source: Fifth National Climate Assessment, chapter 21 (archived), https://web.archive.org/web/20250131234335/https:/nca2023.globalchange.gov/chapter/21, Nov 2023.
- A global rainfall erosivity map built from GPM IMERG and 3,286 stations notes that only 5 percent of stations are in Africa and South America combined. Source: Fenta et al., Scientific Reports, https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-59019-1, 8 Apr 2024.
- From over 130,000 yield records, the extreme heat threshold is 34.8 ± 4.0 °C for maize and 33.7 ± 3.9 °C for soybean, and crop models underestimate it. Source: Nature Food, https://www.nature.com/articles/s43016-026-01298-0, 18 Feb 2026 (abstract only).
- The Rosario grain exchange cut Argentina's 2023 soybean forecast from 47 to 27 million tonnes and corn from 55 to 35 million tonnes, and put the GDP hit at 3 points (US$19 billion). Source: Reuters via US News, https://usnews.com/news/world/articles/2023-03-09/argentinas-unprecedented-drought-pummels-farmers-and-economy, 9 Mar 2023, and https://whtc.com/2023/03/10/historic-drought-in-argentina-seen-shrinking-gdp-by-3-points, 10 Mar 2023.
- Southern African maize output for 2024/25 was 31.5 million tonnes, 16 percent below average. Zambia lost 1.55 million ha of maize, 69 percent of planted area. Source: USDA FAS, https://fas.usda.gov/data/el-nino-drought-reduces-my-202425-corn-production-16-percent-average-southern-africa, 1 Aug 2024.
- February 2024 was the driest February in the 40-year CHIRPS record over much of Zambia and Zimbabwe. Source: NASA Earth Observatory, https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/parched-kafue-flats-152746, 1 May 2024, and repost https://essic.umd.edu/severe-drought-in-southern-africa/, 8 May 2024.
- About 68 million people in southern Africa needed aid. Source: Africanews, https://africanews.com/2024/08/19/el-nino-drought-affects-68-million-in-southern-africa, 19 Aug 2024.
- The UK's hottest and driest spring on record in 2025 cut output of the five main arable crops 20 percent below the 10-year average, costing more than £800 million. Source: The Guardian, https://www.theguardian.com/environment/2025/dec/04/record-heat-drought-2025-cost-uk-arable-farmers-estimated-800m-climate-crisis-grain-harvest, 4 Dec 2025.
- EU maize output of 48.0 million tonnes in 2026 is the smallest since 1994/95, after heat and drought. Source: USDA FAS, https://www.fas.usda.gov/data/european-union-corn-2026-seasonal-update, 30 Oct 2026.
- Kenya's 2026/27 maize output of 2.2 million tonnes is 39 percent below the five-year average after a June to August El Niño drought. Source: USDA FAS, https://fas.usda.gov/data/severe-el-nino-drought-reduces-kenya-202627-corn-production, 15 Oct 2026.
- India's monsoon was 94 percent of the long period average (LPA) in 2023 with August at 64 percent, 108 percent in 2024 (Punjab deficient), 108 percent in 2025 (north-west India wettest since 2001, east and north-east India second lowest since 1901), and 87 percent in 2026. Sources: IMD end-of-season releases https://internal.imd.gov.in/press_release/20231001_pr_2555.pdf (1 Oct 2023), https://internal.imd.gov.in/press_release/20241001_pr_3310.pdf (1 Oct 2024), https://internal.imd.gov.in/press_release/20250930_pr_4343.pdf (30 Sep 2025), https://internal.imd.gov.in/press_release/20261001_pr_5368.pdf (1 Oct 2026).
- USDA expects India's 2026 rabi sowing to shift toward drought-resilient crops after the weak monsoon. Source: USDA FAS GAIN, https://www.fas.usda.gov/data/gain/2026/10/india-weak-southwest-monsoon-and-el-nino-threaten-winter-sowing, 30 Oct 2026.
- Heat-tolerant or biofortified wheat covered 80 percent of wheat area in Punjab and Haryana in 2023-24 after the 2022 heatwave cut yields about 4.5 percent. Source: Hindustan Times, https://hindustantimes.com/india-news/farmers-switch-to-heat-tolerant-wheat-to-fight-climate-crisis-impact-101704827005598.html, 10 Jan 2024.

### Affected regions

The US West and Northeast, the Argentine Pampas, Zambia, Zimbabwe, Malawi, the UK and EU, the Kenyan Rift Valley, and rainfed central, east, and north-east India.

### Where a rotation tool helps and where it cannot

The tool can flag rising heat-day and dry-spell risk in each crop's sensitive stage, such as maize flowering in February in southern Africa or late-sown wheat after late paddy in north India. It can use a longer frost-free window to suggest a double crop or cover crop, and it can score erosion exposure from rain intensity and slope. Argentina lost soy, corn, and wheat in one season, so the tool should show that swapping between similar crops does not remove drought risk. A severe El Niño season can defeat any rotation, and the tool cannot replace insurance, irrigation, or relief. Heat thresholds vary by region, so the tool must show them as local checks.

### NASA evidence link

GPM IMERG gives dry spells, rainfall intensity, and erosivity. SMAP and FLDAS give root-zone moisture. MODIS and VIIRS show vegetation loss. ECOSTRESS shows crop water stress. NASA POWER gives temperature, frost dates, and degree days. SRTM gives slope.

## 6. Africa and smallholder farms

### Summary

African Union leaders at the Nairobi soil health summit in May 2024 pledged that by 2034 at least 70 percent of smallholders will get tailored agronomic advice, including digital decision support. Estimates of soil degradation differ by source, so the tool must say which one it uses. Grain legumes in rotation raise the next maize crop by about 41 percent on average, but the gain disappears under erratic rain or nitrogen leaching, and many farms are too small to rotate and still grow enough maize. Push-pull raises income, though most evidence comes from trials in western Kenya. CGIAR programs show digital advisories can reach millions and that site-specific advice raises yields by 15 to 45 percent.

### Key numbers

- The Nairobi Declaration targets for 2034 include tripling domestic certified fertilizer output and reaching at least 70 percent of smallholders with tailored advice. Source: African Union, http://au.int/en/pressreleases/20240521/africa-pledges-triple-fertilizer-production-distribution-empower-farmers, 21 May 2024.
- AUDA-NEPAD says 75 to 80 percent of cultivated land is degraded, losing 30 to 60 kg of nutrients per hectare each year, affecting 485 million people (65 percent of the population). Source: https://www.nepad.org/publication/briefing-note-addressing-africas-soil-health-challenges-through-ten-year-african, about Dec 2023.
- CGIAR says up to 65 percent of African farmland (494 million ha) is degraded and fertilizer use is 22 kg/ha against a world average of 195 kg/ha. Source: https://cgiar.org/news-events/news/africas-urgent-call-to-improve-soil-health-for-effective-fertilizer-use, 7 May 2024. The two "65 percent" figures measure different things (people versus land).
- A meta-analysis of 199 observations found cereal yield after a grain legume rose 0.49 t/ha (41 percent), more in southern Africa. Source: Franke et al. 2018, https://n2africa.org/sustainable-intensification-through-rotations-grain-legumes-sub-saharan-africa-review (older than 2023 but the core evidence).
- CIMMYT trials on 40 Zambian farms found maize-legume rotation could add 1 to 2 t/ha in Eastern Province but little in Southern Province (erratic rain, grazed residues). Only 35 to 70 percent of farms had enough land to rotate and still meet maize needs. Source: CIMMYT, https://www.cimmyt.org/news/unlocking-zambias-maize-potential-through-crop-diversity/, 24 Jul 2024.
- Climate-resilient push-pull adopters in Kenya and Tanzania earned US$439 to 474 more. Source: Agriculture and Food Security, https://agricultureandfoodsecurity.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s40066-023-00418-4, 8 Jun 2023.
- A 2023 review counts about 280,000 push-pull farmers and finds the evidence concentrated in icipe trials in western Kenya. Source: https://thehive.icipe.org/all-prp/1475, 2023 (abstract only; the farmer count is from a snippet).
- AICCRA reached 1.8 million smallholders in 2024. RiceAdvice raised rice yields 45 percent in Mali, and site-specific fertilizer advice raised Ethiopian yields 24 percent. Source: AICCRA, https://aiccra.cgiar.org/annual-report-2024-evolution-scaling-cgiar-innovation, 2025.

### Affected regions

Sub-Saharan Africa broadly, with rotation work in Zambia, Malawi, and Zimbabwe, push-pull in western Kenya, Uganda, Tanzania, and Ethiopia, and advisories in Ethiopia, Mali, Senegal, Ghana, and Kenya.

### Where a rotation tool helps and where it cannot

The tool can compare continuous maize with maize-legume rotation and push-pull using rainfall reliability, and it can warn small farms when rotation would cut their maize supply below need. It answers the Nairobi pledge for tailored digital advice. It cannot fix access to seed, inoculant, fertilizer, credit, or labour, or stop livestock from grazing residues. African soil and rain station data is sparse, so the tool must flag low confidence.

### NASA evidence link

GPM IMERG and SMAP give rainfall reliability and soil moisture. MODIS, VIIRS, and Landsat give residue cover and maize monoculture history. NASA POWER gives season climate and SRTM gives slope. Soil degradation needs non-NASA data such as iSDAsoil or SoilGrids.

## 7. Farmer adoption of decision tools

### Summary

Farmers adopt digital tools slowly and mainly when the payoff is clear. In June 2026, 52 percent of US farmers said AI or data tools offered no meaningful benefit, and most said AI advice would be hard to follow. McKinsey's surveys show adoption nearly flat since 2022, with agronomists still the most trusted influence. A decade of research finds the same barriers: trust, relevance, usability, cost, and proof that the tool works locally. In India, farmers use weather advisories for sowing and spraying but not for choosing crops, and extension workers remain central to trust. India's AgriStack now holds more than 10 crore farmer IDs, which gives a rotation tool a possible data path.

### Key numbers

- In a June 2026 survey of 400 farmers, 52 percent saw no meaningful benefit from AI or data tools, 23 percent named higher production, and 63 percent said AI recommendations would sometimes be hard to follow. Source: Purdue/CME Ag Economy Barometer, https://ag.purdue.edu/commercialag/ageconomybarometer/media-high-input-cost-concerns, 7 Jul 2026 (full URL slug not confirmed; see source log).
- McKinsey 2024 (about 4,400 farmers in 9 countries): digital tool use rose only 3 points since 2022. 53 percent of North American farmers want clear proof of return first. Crop rotation is the most common sustainable practice at 68 percent, and about 60 percent of US farmers see carbon program returns as too low. Source: McKinsey (Brazil mirror), https://mckinsey.com.br/industries/agriculture/our-insights/global-farmer-insights-2024, 16 Oct 2024.
- McKinsey 2026 (5,500 farmers in 10 countries): 50 percent use at least one agtech, 56 percent name an agronomist as a top influence, and 6 percent trust AI. Source: AGDAILY summary, https://agdaily.com/technology/agtech-adoption-grows-farmers-want-proof-investing, 15 Sep 2026 (McKinsey original not read).
- Auto-steer is on 52 percent of midsize and 70 percent of large US crop farms, and small farms have the lowest use of precision tools. Source: USDA ERS, https://ers.usda.gov/data-products/charts-of-note/110550, 10 Dec 2024.
- A UK study found 395 decision tools on the market but only 49 percent of farmers used any. It lists 15 adoption factors, including trust, peer recommendation, and relevance. Source: Rose et al. 2016, Agricultural Systems, https://ueaeprints.uea.ac.uk/60758/1/Published_manuscript.pdf.
- A survey of more than 2,000 certified crop advisers found they prefer tools that are simple, use satellite data with few manual inputs, can be calibrated locally, and let users edit the recommendation. Source: University of Vermont, https://www.uvm.edu/cals/news/farm-advisors-trust-their-gut-over-algorithms-now, 25 Feb 2026.
- India's Digital Agriculture Mission has Rs 2,817 crore and names crop diversification as a goal. Source: PIB, https://pib.gov.in/Pressreleaseshare.aspx?PRID=2050966, 2 Sep 2024.
- AgriStack farmer IDs passed 10.31 crore by 3 August 2026, and the rabi 2025-26 digital crop survey covered more than 31.3 crore plots. Source: PIB, https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2296209, 7 Aug 2026.
- Kisan e-Mitra, a voice AI chatbot in 11 languages, has answered more than 95 lakh queries from more than 53 lakh farmers. Source: PIB, https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2244630, 24 Mar 2026.
- Krishi-DSS, launched in August 2024, offers parcel-level crop maps across years for rotation analysis. Source: PIB, https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleseDetailm.aspx?PRID=2045987, 16 Aug 2024.
- In an ICRISAT study of 200 Odisha farmers, 67 percent used advisories for sowing and 69 percent for crop protection, but farmers did not use them for crop or variety choice. Source: ICRISAT, https://oar.icrisat.org/12920/, 2024 (abstract only).

### What this means for the tool

Lead with expected returns per acre and their range. Make every suggestion explained and editable, and show its evidence and how well it has been checked locally. Pull satellite data automatically so the farmer types little. Present the tool as a way to prepare for a talk with an agronomist, with a printable summary. Keep farm data local or shared only with consent.

### NASA evidence link

This topic is about design, not data. The surveys support the challenge's own rule that the tool supports and never replaces farmer judgment.

## 8. Farm economics, inputs, insurance, and carbon markets

### Summary

US farm profits are falling after inflation, the median farm household earns a small loss from farming, and recent farm income leans heavily on emergency aid. China bought almost no US soybeans for about six months in 2025, and the deal that followed sets purchases below the 2020 to 2024 average. Fertilizer prices rose again in 2026 after the Iran war, with anhydrous ammonia at about $1,000 per ton in November, and farmdoc suggests more soybean acres to cut fertilizer cost. Crop insurance allows cover crops if farmers end them by NRCS rules. Soil carbon markets are mixed: Nori shut down in 2024 while Indigo has issued over 2 million tonnes of credits, and measurement remains the core doubt.

### Key numbers

- USDA forecasts 2026 net farm income at $158.4 billion, down 5.5 percent after inflation, and median farm household income from farming at minus $467. Source: USDA ERS, https://www.ers.usda.gov/topics/farm-economy/farm-sector-income-finances/highlights-from-the-farm-income-forecast, 3 Sep 2026.

- US soybean sales to China fell to about 18 million tonnes in 2025, down 33 percent from 2024. The deal's 25 million tonnes a year is 14 percent below the 2020 to 2024 average. Source: farmdoc daily, https://farmdocdaily.illinois.edu/2025/11/us-china-soybean-deal-comparing-past-export-levels-and-global-market-impacts.html, 17 Nov 2025.
- Illinois anhydrous ammonia cost $915.50 per ton in August 2026, up 16 percent on 2025, after peaking above $1,100 in spring. farmdoc suggests shifting acres toward soybeans for 2027. - In October 2026, 62 percent of farmers said they had a strong balance sheet, down from 76 percent in January, and high input costs were the top concern. Source: Purdue/CME, https://ag.purdue.edu/commercialag/ageconomybarometer/optimism-grows-in-october-as-current-conditions-improve, 3 Nov 2026.

Source: Yale School of the Environment on Potash et al. 2025, https://environment.yale.edu/news/article/direct-measurements-can-reduce-uncertainty-soil-carbon-credit-markets, 17 Jan 2025.

### Affected regions

Corn Belt soybean exporters (Iowa, Illinois, Minnesota, Nebraska, the Dakotas) and nitrogen-heavy corn areas.

### Where a rotation tool helps and where it cannot

The tool can rerun rotation budgets under current fertilizer, fuel, and price scenarios, show how much a rotation depends on one export market, and check cover crop plans against termination rules. It can give a soil carbon range clearly labelled as uncertain. It cannot predict trade deals, war-driven price spikes, or emergency aid, and it should never quote carbon credit values.

### NASA evidence link

NASA POWER and GPM IMERG supply weather risk for budget scenarios. Landsat and MODIS crop history support records of rotation and cover crops. Remote sensing can verify a practice but cannot measure soil carbon change.

### 2. Crop-switching paper (32, 39, and 140 percent): revised, and the numbers do not apply to Haryana

- Citation: Chakraborti R., Davis K.F., DeFries R., Rao N.D., Joseph J., Ghosh S. "Crop switching for water sustainability in India's food bowl yields co-benefits for food security and farmers' profits." Nature Water 1, 864 to 878 (2023). DOI 10.1038/s44221-023-00135-z. https://www.nature.com/articles/s44221-023-00135-z, 9 Oct 2023 (read in full; CC BY 4.0).
- The study covers only Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, and West Bengal (124 districts in the Indo-Gangetic Plain). It names Punjab and Haryana only as depletion hotspots. The tool must not present these numbers as Haryana results.
- The headline figures (32 percent less water, 39 percent more calories, 140 percent more profit) combine both seasons. The switches are rice to pearl millet and sorghum in kharif and wheat to sorghum and barley in rabi.
- It is a district-level optimisation with cereal area held constant, using 2014-15 data and assuming all output sells at MSP. Capping the cut in rice area at 20 or 40 percent shrinks the gains a lot.

### 3. NASA DAPI paper (SMAP forecasts NDVI about 30 days ahead): confirmed with a revised lead time

- Citation: Le M.-H., Bolten J.D., Whitney K.M., Johnson D.M., Mueller R., Mladenova I.E. "On the Use of SMAP Soil Moisture for Forecasting NDVI Over CONUS Cropland Regions." Geophysical Research Letters 51(20), e2024GL111187, 15 Oct 2024. DOI 10.1029/2024GL111187. NASA record https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20240012812 (read in full) and supplement https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20240012812/downloads/2024_Le_GRL_SuppInfo.pdf (read in full). The main PDF text would not extract, so we read its abstract and key points only.
- Inputs: root-zone soil moisture from the USDA Palmer model with SMAP assimilation at 10 km, April 2015 to December 2021, and VIIRS NDVI (not MODIS) at 16-day steps.
- Region: the contiguous US only, for corn, soybean, cotton, and wheat. It has not been tested in India.
- Lead times are 16, 32, and 48 days. At 16 days the forecast beat an NDVI climatology for all four crops. At about 32 days it beat climatology only for cotton and wheat. Gains were largest in water-limited areas and in floods and droughts.

### 4. Maize uses about 80 percent less water than rice, and rice 1,500 mm, maize 500 mm, wheat 400 mm, mustard 250 mm: partly confirmed, needs relabelling

- Irrigation applied on farms in Karnal, Kurukshetra, and Ambala (250 farmers, kharif 2021 to 2023): maize got 3.2 irrigations against 30.8 for rice, about 90 percent less irrigation water. Maize net returns were 46.5 percent higher. Source: Jat et al., Frontiers in Sustainable Food Systems, https://frontiersin.org/journals/sustainable-food-systems/articles/10.3389/fsufs.2025.1700854/full, 5 Dec 2025 (read in full).
- Crop water demand (ET) in Karnal and Kaithal falls 54.66 percent per hectare when maize replaces rice. Source: Rajput et al. 2024, Water Supply (abstract only, via Consensus).
- In Punjab, annual ET is 92 cm for rice-wheat and 86 cm for maize-wheat, but irrigation need is 106.8 cm against 61.2 cm, because most extra rice water drains below the roots. Source: Jalota et al. 2002, Agricultural Water Management (abstract only).
- Kharif maize needs 3 to 4 irrigations, while spring maize needs about 15 to 18. Source: Indian Express, https://indianexpress.com/article/cities/chandigarh/maize-double-edged-sword-punjab-groundwater-crisis-9931507, 8 Apr 2025 (read in full).

Mustard 250 mm sits inside a 190 to 400 mm range (ICAR, snippet only). The tool must label each figure as ET or irrigation.
