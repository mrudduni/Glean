# Problems most worth targeting

Ranking made on 2026-11-14 from [news_and_reports.md](news_and_reports.md) and [nasa_agriculture_news_2024_2026.md](nasa_agriculture_news_2024_2026.md). Every number below is cited in those files with its URL and date. The scores are our judgment, not a formula from the judges.

## How we scored

Each problem gets 1 to 5 on four criteria.

- Severity: how large, fast, and widely felt the harm is, from the evidence we read.
- Rotation leverage: how much a farmer's rotation choice changes the outcome. A low score means policy, markets, or weather dominate.
- NASA data fit: how directly the challenge's NASA datasets measure the problem at a scale a farmer can use.
- Story strength: how clearly the problem maps to the judges' Impact and Relevance scores in a 30-second video or 7 slides.

The total is out of 20. Ties are broken by story strength.

## Rationale

### 1. Groundwater collapse under rice-wheat in Punjab and Haryana (20)

- Severity: Punjab draws 156 percent of recharge and Haryana 137 percent (CGWB 2025). All 22 blocks in Karnal, Kurukshetra, and Kaithal are over-exploited, and Kurukshetra district is at 228 percent (CGWB 2024).
- Rotation leverage: paddy is the water sink, and both states already pay farmers to switch (Rs 8,000 per acre under MPMV in Haryana, Rs 17,500 per hectare for maize in Punjab). Farm data from Karnal and Kurukshetra shows kharif maize used about 90 percent less irrigation water than rice, with higher net returns.
- NASA data fit: this is where NASA's groundwater story began. Rodell et al. (2009) used GRACE to measure 109 km³ lost from 2002 to 2008, and later GRACE-FO work extends the record to 2021 and to Haryana districts. SMAP, IMERG, and ET products cover the field side.
- Story strength: it is our chosen demo region, and the gap is clear. Fewer than 20 percent of MPMV's 2025 target acres qualified, and Haryana paddy area grew 35 percent in ten years, so farmers need a reason to trust a switch, not just a payment.
- Limits to state openly: MSP purchase guarantees for paddy and free power drive most choices, and maize MSP is weak in practice (HAFED bought no maize from 2022-23 to 2024-25). The tool must not claim that rotation advice alone will fix the aquifer.

### 2. Ogallala depletion in Kansas and Texas (19)

- Severity: 286 million acre-feet of storage is gone (USGS 2024), and parts of southwest Kansas fell more than a foot in 2024.
- Rotation leverage: SD-6 is peer-reviewed proof that farmer-chosen cuts work: 25 to 31 percent less water with incomes held. Part of the gain came from moving corn and soybean to sorghum and wheat.
- NASA data fit: OpenET and Landsat fit well at field scale. GRACE shows the region but not a farm. We found no strong recent High Plains GRACE paper, so the fit scores 4.
- Story strength: high. Kansas had action plans due in July 2026, and one was rejected in September 2026. The US story also helps for judges outside India.

### 3. Erratic monsoon and in-season heat (17)

- Severity: India's monsoon went from 94 to 108 to 108 to 87 percent of normal over 2023 to 2026, with large regional swings. Heat cut wheat yields in 2022 and pushed rapid uptake of heat-tolerant varieties.
- Rotation leverage: moderate. Crop and sowing-date choice changes exposure, but a bad season can defeat any rotation.
- NASA data fit: excellent. GPM IMERG, SMAP, NASA POWER, MODIS LST, and ECOSTRESS measure this directly, with long records.
- Story strength: good, and it ties to the challenge text's "shifting temperatures" and "changing rainfall". It works best as a risk layer on top of problem 1, not as a separate pitch.

### 4. Corn-soybean monoculture in the Midwest (17)

- Severity: the A horizon is gone from about 35 percent of Corn Belt cropland (Thaler et al. 2021, snippet only), and the Gulf dead zone stays about twice its goal.
- Rotation leverage: very high. Marsden Farm shows longer rotations matched profit while cutting synthetic nitrogen and herbicide by more than 80 percent.
- NASA data fit: good. SRTM slope with IMERG rain intensity gives erosion risk, and Landsat shows cover and crop history. The Cropland Data Layer gives rotation history.
- Story strength: solid but familiar to judges. 2025 cuts to conservation staff and climate programs add urgency.

### 5. Drought and soil decline on African smallholdings (17)

- Severity: the 2024 drought destroyed 69 percent of Zambia's maize area, Kenya's 2026/27 maize crop is 39 percent below average, and up to 65 percent of African farmland is degraded (CGIAR).
- Rotation leverage: legume rotation raises the next maize crop by about 41 percent on average, but the gain fades under erratic rain, and many farms are too small to rotate.
- NASA data fit: satellites are often the only data, but station data for checking them is sparse.
- Story strength: strong for Impact, and the African Union pledged digital advice for 70 percent of smallholders by 2034. The tool would need different crops, units, and channels than the Haryana demo.

### 6. Late paddy harvest, short wheat window, and stubble burning (16)

- Severity: Haryana fire counts fell from 6,987 (2021) to 662 (2025), but Punjab's burnt area did not fall in 2024 even as fire counts dropped 70 percent.
- Rotation leverage: real. Short-duration paddy, direct seeding, or a maize kharif widen the gap before wheat.
- NASA data fit: MODIS and VIIRS fire products and Landsat burnt area fit, but single-pass fire counts miss fires.
- Story strength: high in India and well known abroad through Delhi's air. It fits as a feature of problem 1 (a "bare field and burning risk" flag), which the challenge text invites with "times when fields are typically bare".

### 7. SGMA in the Central Valley (16)

- Severity: 500,000 to 900,000 acres may leave irrigated production by the early 2040s (PPIC).
- Rotation leverage: limited. Much of the land is in almonds and pistachios, where change is an orchard decision. Winter forage on fallow land is the main rotation lever.
- NASA data fit: excellent. The state reports GRACE data and uses OpenET in SGMA planning.
- Story strength: good, but the main story is policy forcing fallowing, not farmer choice.

### 8. Fertilizer price swings and thin margins (13)

- Severity: US retail anhydrous ammonia cost about $1,000 per ton in November 2026, up 17 percent on a year earlier, and real farm income is falling.
- Rotation leverage: legumes cut nitrogen bills, and farmdoc suggests more soybean acres for 2027.
- NASA data fit: weak. NASA data does not measure prices.
- Story strength: moderate. It is best used as a "money" axis in the comparison table, since surveys show farmers adopt tools only when returns are clear.

## Problems we did not rank

- Soil carbon markets: the measurement doubts and the 2024 Nori shutdown make it a poor primary target. The tool can show a labelled soil carbon range.
- Low adoption of decision tools: this is a design constraint, not a target. It shapes how the tool must present results (see section 7 of news_and_reports.md).

## What this means for the pitch

- Lead with problem 1 in Karnal, Kurukshetra, and Kaithal. Use problem 6 as a feature and problem 3 as the risk layer.
- Use problem 2 as the second demo or the "works elsewhere" slide, because SD-6 gives peer-reviewed proof that crop switching saves water while incomes hold.
- Every water saving shown must state whether it is ET or irrigation applied, since the two differ by a factor of about two for maize against rice.
- Do not show the Nature Water figures (32 percent less water, 140 percent more profit) as Haryana results. They come from Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, and West Bengal.
